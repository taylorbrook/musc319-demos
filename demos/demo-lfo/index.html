One LFO, many destinations

A low-frequency oscillator (LFO) is one slow oscillator, well below hearing range, whose output you use as a control signal rather than a sound. Set its rate, depth, and shape once, then choose which parameter it drives. Pointed at pitch it is vibrato; at the filter it is a sweep; at volume it is tremolo; at pan it is auto-pan. Same source, four names. Press Start, then switch the destination and listen: the LFO never changes, only where it is routed.

Try this: leave the rate near 5 Hz and depth near 40%, then step through the four destinations. The rate you hear as a "wobble speed" is identical every time; only the parameter it moves is different. Then raise the depth and slow the rate to hear each effect exaggerated.
