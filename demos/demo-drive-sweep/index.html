Drive sweep: clean, saturated, hard-clipped

A sustained sawtooth is pushed into a fixed tanh transfer curve. Raise the drive and the tone goes from clean, through soft saturation that thickens it, into hard clipping that makes it buzz and growl. The output level is gain-compensated, so what you hear change is the harmonic content, not the loudness. Watch the spectrum: harmonics fill in while the pitch stays fixed. Press Start, then Play.

Step 2: the drive

Around 1x the sawtooth passes almost unchanged. The middle of the range rounds the peaks (soft saturation, low-order harmonics, warmth). Near the top the waveform is flattened against a ceiling (hard clipping, harsh high-order harmonics).

The spectrum (live FFT, 0-10 kHz)

What to watch: the leftmost peak is the fixed fundamental; it never moves. As the drive rises, harmonics stack up above it, stronger and higher as the clipping hardens, while the fundamental stays put. That is added harmonic content at a fixed pitch, the mechanism behind every saturator.
